# Sprint 3 demo — quote reference
Pulled from the real interview transcripts for quick reference while presenting. Not a script — just sourced evidence to speak from.

## Attendance (lead pain point — strongest evidence)

**Mike** (Gups/Pups/Super Pups), on attendance:
> "I should be better about it... There's a clipboard laying around that's been in the kitchen that I did for September to have a bunch of checks on it and I stopped using that... I need to figure out what system works."

**Will** (Senior 2), on his own duct-taped system:
> "I have an Excel file that I have... I'll log the yardage of every practice, the intensity, what we did, and so it will spit out into a chart how many yards each swimmer has done that week... And based on their attendance, how much they attend, like their attendance percentage, and it's all in one place, which is really helpful. And then also for ABSC, we have an app called Sports Engine that we use for billing and everything, and so we take attendance on that as well."
*(Two separate systems, duplicated effort — exactly what the new attendance feature replaces.)*

## Inconsistent technique across groups (widest evidence — 4+ coaches, unprompted)

**Adam** (Senior 1, 20+ years coaching):
> "It's like, Adam does this, Jonathan does that... we got to get on the same page of what are we teaching? We got to teach the same way... we got to have a common goal."

**Will** (Senior 2):
> "I think like a more uniform strategy for like even in like technical skills that people need to like streamlines like their, how their strokes look."

**Lydia**, on her own incremental technique-checklist approach (streamline off every wall, worked until ingrained before moving on):
> "I've never heard of a coach doing it like that, but it actually kind of sounds like a good idea."

*(Caveat if asked: the proposed fix — Adam's "coaches retreat" — is a human process, not obviously software. Real pain, ambiguous build path.)*

## Practice-timing arithmetic (already solved — confirms Sprint 2 work mattered)

**Will** (first year writing his own practices):
> "The most annoying part is the timing. So like how long a set will take, how long the whole practice will take... doing a bunch of arithmetic is very fun."

**Contrast — Adam** (20+ years), no sign of this pain at all:
> "I traditionally don't really write down a season plan. I train and adapt as I watch them train and adapt."

*(This pain is concentrated in newer coaches, not universal — and it's the exact problem the deterministic-duration feature from Sprint 2 already solves.)*

## Goal tracking — vitamin for senior groups, closer to a pill for younger ones

**Will**, goals are athlete-owned:
> "Tracking goals, that's mainly an athlete thing. I know what they want to do kind of and I kind of let them come to me with goals that they want."

**Mike**, contrast — goals are coach- and parent-driven for younger swimmers:
> "We usually have a goal setting document... I'll send them like, 'Here's what it would take to get third place at divisionals, here's what it would take to make the B state, the A state.'"
> "I also have like a spreadsheet that I send the parents that has a bunch of qualifying plans on it... so that they can kind of know if their kids are getting close to qualifying times."

## Foundational framing (from the first conversation with John, still holds)

> "You're not selling to, like, one coach at a club. You're trying to get a club to adopt." The pitch is "setting up an interaction between a parent and somebody at a club that uses your product" — what can a coach tell a parent *because* they use this.

## Known gaps — say these directly, don't dodge them
- Jonathan (head coach) hasn't been interviewed yet.
- No parent evidence yet — everything so far is from coaches.
- The "club would pay for consistency" leap is a hypothesis, not confirmed — nobody with budget authority was interviewed.
